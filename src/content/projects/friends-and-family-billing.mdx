## The math was never the problem

My husband and I split T-Mobile, Apple One, and 1Password across a household of eight people. Every year the same thing happened: someone would ask how much they owed, I would open a spreadsheet, re-derive the math from three billing cadences, and text them a number. Then someone else would ask, and I would do it again.

The arithmetic was trivial. The product problem was the coordination wrapped around it—calculate, explain, ask, remind, verify, repeat—and its social texture: asking for money feels awkward, checking feels intrusive, and both jobs default to whoever set the thing up.

That loop holds two users with different needs. The coordinator needs accuracy and an auditable record—I am the one vouching for the numbers. A recipient needs an amount, the arithmetic behind it, a way to pay, and a way to object, with no account in the way. Friends & Family Billing holds those two jobs apart.

## One link is the recipient's whole year

A household member's entire relationship with this product is a URL. Its token is hashed with SHA-256 to key a cached document scoped to that one member, and the page it opens is built around one choice: show the arithmetic, not just the answer. Every bill renders its derivation inline—`$300.00/mo ÷ 8 members = $37.50/mo · ×12 = $450.00/yr`—and nothing in that line is recomputed for display: each figure reads from the canonical fields the billing math wrote, so the page cannot imply a rounding the calculation never took. The invoice email, by contrast, carries a greeting, a total, and the link—the email asks, the page explains.

A recipient can do four things there, none requiring an account: question a specific charge, approve or reject the resolution I propose, confirm or deny receipt of a refund, and request a fresh link when theirs has died. They cannot record a payment—no such control or write scope exists. The recipient sees, questions, and confirms; only the coordinator posts money.

The question path shows the mechanism under a no-login page. "Question This Charge" posts to a Cloud Function rather than writing the database directly, and the function adds an authorization check the security rules never make—it rejects the dispute unless the token's member is actually on that bill—then rate-limits per token, writes a server-side audit entry, and emails me the question with a deep link into my review queue. Six of the seven Cloud Functions exist to serve someone who has no account.

Three coordination steps are verifiably gone. The recipient learns their amount and its derivation without asking me. I learn whether they have looked without asking—every link carries a visit count and a last-viewed date I can read back. And a dead link recovers itself: the expired page offers "Request New Link," and the system emails me the request. A fourth step is not gone: nothing sends on its own—there is no scheduler in this codebase. Once a year I still open a dialog and press Send, per member. The product removed the explaining and the checking, not the asking.

## The decisions

Four decisions carry the argument, and all four read mixed—real support beside a real limitation. Two are about money and access: who may see a household's numbers, and what the model owes the person who overpaid. Two come from the weeks the rendering model broke, told in [Six PRs, One Bug](/blog/six-prs-one-bug-agent-failure-modes/): what the fix actually fixed, and how the work was run differently after three fixes had failed.

## The boundary is possession, not identity

No record weighs links against accounts—no ADR, spec, or pull request carries that deliberation. The tradeoff was my product judgment, and the honest way to explain it is to show the mechanism.

The rule guarding the public share document is `allow read: if true`. No authentication term, no expiry comparison, no revocation check; as written it even permits enumerating the collection. What stands between the world and a household's numbers is possession of the token—a 256-bit preimage problem, not an access-control decision—and the application enforces expiry and revocation itself, once in the browser and again in the Cloud Function.

The strongest half of the design is what revocation does: it deletes the cached document. That matters because the read rule is unconditional—flipping a flag would not stop a direct reader; removing the document does. Minting a new link also prunes each member to their five most recent, deleting the rest.

The limits sit next to that. An expired link's cached document is refused by the application but never deleted. The raw token is stored server-side—deliberately, so a sent link can be re-copied—guarded by owner-only rules rather than by not existing. The token rides in the query string, so it survives in hosting logs, browser history, and whatever message carried it. And the no-login visitor is not anonymous to me: every server-side resolution records the caller's IP into an audit log only I can read.

## What the record shows, and what it does not

The repository holds no usage record—no seed data, no exported dataset, and analytics that instrument sign-in and nothing else. One household has used this product; the only numbers that could substantiate how much live in the running system's per-link view counters, not in the source. What the repository can show is the mechanism and the care taken with it, not adoption.

One real datum survives, as a test fixture: one member's 2026 annual summary of $1,069.76, already paid—the exact strings from the debugging arc's reference screenshot, preserved to pin a renderer. It is the only trace of real use the source carries.

## What it demonstrates

The product ended one recurring conversation, on my own account of it. What the record demonstrates is narrower. The brief that ended the rendering arc defined done in four clauses; the two turned into automated checks have held since April 2026, and the two that stayed prose—an editor that reflects final output, tests spanning every surface—were not met at merge and are not met today. The invoice email's divergence is the same finding from the other side: the surface with a check stayed fixed; the surface without one drifted the day the fix landed. An invariant that is prose is an invariant nobody reviews.
